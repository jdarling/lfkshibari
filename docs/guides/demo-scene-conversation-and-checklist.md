# Demo Scene Conversation and Checklist

Use this for short, same-day scenes where at least one person is experienced and the conversation needs to be concise without skipping important consent, comfort, and safety topics.

This can be useful for classes, demonstrations, practice sessions, mini scenes, skill exchanges, or other situations where people may be tying together shortly after meeting.

The goal is not to recreate a full compatibility conversation. It is to establish enough shared understanding to decide whether the scene makes sense, what the scene will include, and whether both people are ready to proceed.

---

## Introductions

Keep introductions short and relevant to the scene.

### Rigger

**Example introduction**

> Hi, I'm ________. I've been tying for about ________. For this scene I was thinking about ________. I'm comfortable with ________, and anything outside that is something I would want to talk through before we start.

Useful things to cover:

- What name would you like me to use?
- What pronouns do you use?
- How long have you been tying?
- What are you comfortable tying in this setting?
- Is any part of the planned scene practice, new, or experimental for you?

### Bunny

**Example introduction**

> Hi, I'm ________. I've been getting tied for about ________. I'm comfortable with ________, and I would like to avoid ________. I'll let you know if anything changes or feels wrong.

Useful things to cover:

- What name would you like me to use?
- What pronouns do you use?
- How long have you been getting tied?
- What kinds of rope are you comfortable with?
- Is there anything you already know you do not want in this scene?

---

## Scene Intention

Keep the scope clear and specific.

- What does the bunny want to experience?
- What does the rigger want to demonstrate, practice, or explore?
- What specific ties or techniques are planned?
- Is the scene primarily:
  - Demonstration?
  - Practice?
  - Education?
  - Connection?
  - Sensation?
  - Restraint?
  - Aesthetics?
  - Movement?
  - Something else?
- How intense should the scene feel?
- Is anything new being introduced?
- Is anything specifically excluded?

---

## Current Physical Condition

These questions are about how each person is doing right now.

- Any current injuries?
- Any old injuries likely to matter for this scene?
- Any numbness, tingling, weakness, or nerve concerns?
- Any bruising, cuts, skin irritation, recent piercings, or sensitive areas?
- Any joint, mobility, or range-of-motion limitations?
- Any soreness, fatigue, dehydration, illness, stress, or other current condition that matters?
- Any positions that should be avoided?

---

## Touch and Rope Boundaries

### Without Rope

- Where is touch okay?
- Where is touch not okay?
- Is incidental contact okay in areas where deliberate touch is not?
- Is hands-on positioning okay?
- Is lifting, supporting, catching, or moving your body okay?

### With Rope

- Where is rope okay?
- Where is rope not okay?
- Are there areas where rope can cross but should not apply pressure?
- Are there areas especially sensitive to pressure?
- Is rope on bare skin okay?
- Are there ties, positions, or forms of restriction that are off limits?

---

## Communication

Agree on communication before starting.

- How do you prefer to communicate discomfort?
- Do you prefer frequent check-ins or fewer interruptions?
- Do you tend to get quiet when focused, uncomfortable, or overwhelmed?
- Are there signs I should watch for if you are having trouble communicating?
- What word or signal means:
  - Slow down?
  - Change something?
  - Stop this part?
  - End the scene?
- Are you comfortable stopping the scene without needing to explain why?

---

## Space

- [ ] Tying area is clear
- [ ] Floor is clear of objects, spills, trip hazards, and sharp items
- [ ] There is enough room to move safely
- [ ] Barrier or mat is in place, if needed
- [ ] Furniture or anchor points have been checked, if used
- [ ] Working area is clear of unnecessary traffic
- [ ] Exit and walkway are clear
- [ ] Lighting is sufficient to monitor rope placement and body condition

---

## Rope and Equipment

- [ ] Rope has been inspected
- [ ] Rope is clean and appropriate for use
- [ ] Damaged or questionable rope has been removed
- [ ] Cutter is immediately accessible
- [ ] Cutter location has been shown
- [ ] Suspension hardware has been inspected, if used
- [ ] Mats, padding, or support equipment are ready, if used
- [ ] Everything expected to be needed is within reach

---

## Rigger

- [ ] Comfortable with the planned ties or techniques
- [ ] Any practice or experimental elements have been identified
- [ ] Cutter is immediately accessible
- [ ] Pockets are empty of anything that could cause injury
- [ ] Phone, watch, jewelry, keys, or other items are handled as needed
- [ ] Rope and equipment have been checked

---

## Bunny

- [ ] Pockets are empty
- [ ] Jewelry and accessories are handled as needed
- [ ] Clothing works for the planned scene
- [ ] Current injuries, soreness, numbness, tingling, bruising, or limitations have been disclosed
- [ ] Cutter location is known
- [ ] Communication signals are understood
- [ ] Comfortable with the planned ties and level of intensity

---

## Both

- [ ] We understand the purpose and scope of the scene
- [ ] We have discussed what is included
- [ ] We have discussed what is excluded
- [ ] We have discussed current physical concerns
- [ ] We have confirmed touch boundaries
- [ ] We have confirmed rope boundaries
- [ ] We know how to communicate slow down, change, stop, or end
- [ ] We both feel physically ready
- [ ] We both feel mentally ready

## After the Scene

A short scene still deserves a clear ending.

- Check for numbness, tingling, weakness, unusual pain, or other concerns after the rope comes off.
- Ask whether help getting up or moving is wanted.
- Offer water, space, conversation, or other simple aftercare as appropriate.
- If something felt wrong or unexpected, talk about it before either person moves on to another scene.
