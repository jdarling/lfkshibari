About LFKShibari

Come curious. Grow together.

LFKShibari brings people in Lawrence and the surrounding area together around Shibari, kinbaku, rope bondage, and rope-adjacent practice. Our community is centered on safety, consent, inclusivity, creativity, and respect.

We welcome different roles, experience levels, bodies, identities, relationship structures, styles, and approaches to Shibari. It is both a skill and an art form: bring your own ideas, questions, and curiosity. Practice and experiment with informed consent, communication, and respect for boundaries and the risks involved.

Community over competition means exchanging knowledge, troubleshooting together, encouraging one another, and celebrating progress. Everyone contributes to keeping the space welcoming and supporting responsible practice. Come curious, communicate openly, respect boundaries, and leave your ego at the door.

We do not treat admission, experience, or reputation as a guarantee that someone is safe. Community trust is built through behavior, accountability, and continued respect for consent.

Community standards

What we expect from each other.

01

Consent is active

Consent should be informed, specific, ongoing, and freely given. It can be changed or withdrawn at any time.

02

Privacy matters

Do not out community members, share identifying information, or photograph people without their permission.

03

Skill has limits

Be honest about your experience. Practice within your current knowledge and seek instruction before increasing complexity or risk.

04

Accountability matters

Mistakes and incidents should be addressed, not hidden. Listen, communicate, learn, and change behavior when needed.

05

Newcomers deserve care

Experience can create power imbalances. We expect experienced members to treat new people with patience and without pressure.

06

Community is participatory

Respect the venue, the organizers, the people around you, and the boundaries of scenes you are not part of.

Consent & risk

Rope is not risk-free.

Shibari and rope bondage can involve physical and emotional risk, including nerve compression, circulation problems, falls, strains, and unexpected emotional responses. Education, communication, preparation, and sound technique can reduce risk. They cannot eliminate it.

Everyone participating in rope is expected to communicate honestly, respect limits, stay attentive to changes, and have an appropriate plan for stopping or responding when something goes wrong.

NegotiateDiscuss intent, boundaries, risks, signals, and relevant limitations before starting.
Check inConsent and physical condition can change during a scene. Stay engaged and responsive.
Be preparedHave suitable emergency cutting equipment accessible and know when to stop.
Stay capableImpairment and meaningful consent do not mix. Participate with a clear head.

Frequently asked

A few things worth knowing.

Do I need rope experience?

No. Newcomers are welcome. Be honest about what you know and what you do not know, and work within your current skill level.

Do I have to tie or be tied?

No. Attendance never creates an obligation to participate in rope, accept touch, enter a scene, or explain a boundary.

Can I take photos?

Only with permission from everyone who may be identifiable. Permission to take a photo is separate from permission to post or share it.

Does vetting mean someone is safe?

No. Vetting is an admission process based on information available at the time. Every person remains responsible for their own negotiations, boundaries, choices, and risk decisions.
